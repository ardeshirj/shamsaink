ShamsaInk layouts

While you read, the screen holds the page and nothing else. Everything else comes to your hand: a tap brings the dock to the nearest edge, a held margin shows where you are, a held word offers what can be done with it, and the page buttons reach all of it. Every control is a layer over the engine's page, which is never written to. Drawn for the Kobo Libra Colour (1264 × 1680, 7 inches, two page buttons, colour, Bluetooth audio), the first target; nothing in them is Kobo's alone.

Each layer is named by its waveform and when it is refreshed.

Principles

readingThe page alone

No clock, no bar, no ticking: the reader sleeps until a touch or a button.

reachControls come to the hand

The dock at the nearest edge; the glance beside the finger; in landscape, at the grip.

devicesWhat the device has, only

Light where there is a light, Listen where there is sound, colour on colour screens. Nothing greyed out.

buttonsEverything by buttons too

The page buttons reach every control, for devices and people that need them.

e-inkRefresh what changed

Each layer its own box and waveform; black and white controls, no shadows, greys or motion.

The page buttons

Forward is the lower button whichever way the device is held (the accelerometer says which). A press acts at once; a hold is half a second.

Ideas for the reading

behaviourSmarter flashes

We hold the old page and the new one: count how much ink changed, and flash only when ghosting would show (after a picture, a heavy page), not every N pages.

behaviourYou were here

Places kept by word, not page. On reopening, and after a text size change reflows the pages, the word you were at is underlined for a moment.

behaviourTime left at your pace

"12 min left in chapter" learnt from how fast you turn pages in this book, not a fixed rate.

settingA reading ruler

An optional underline under the current line, moved by the buttons line by line: for dense classical text, and for dyslexia.

settingWarmth follows the day

On devices with warm light, warmer in the evening by the clock, back in the morning.

to checkKashida

Persian lines justified by stretching letters, as Persian has long been set, not by widening spaces. Whether Blitz can, to check first.

to checkA dictionary

For Farsi classics (Attar, Rumi, Bayhaqi) a gloss of old words is what makes them readable; English too. An offline dictionary's licence, to find.
